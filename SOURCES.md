# Sources and Reading Guide

This is a working bibliography, not a substitute for specialist bibliographic review. Sources are grouped by how they are used in the project.

## Linear A script, sign values, and methodology

### Ester Salgarella

**Linear A** (Cambridge Elements, 2023).

Useful for the relationship between Linear A and Linear B, the cautious backward projection of sound values, shared place/name sequences, and current methodological framing.

Repository/search starting point: https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/

### Brent Davis

**The Undeciphered Aegean Scripts** (Cambridge University Press, 2026), especially chapters on Linear A morphology, syntax, and orthography.

Project use: combinatorial/contextual methodology; statistical identification of probable affixal behavior; regional/scribal orthographic controls. Chapter 4 explicitly isolates a small set of signs highly likely to behave as prefixes/suffixes and identifies one suffix as especially compatible with genitive/ablative/toponymic-adjective function.

Publisher: https://www.cambridge.org/

### Rose Thomas

“Some reflections on morphology in the language of the Linear A libation formula” (Kadmos, 2020).

Project use: morphological analysis of recurrent ritual/libation-formula variants and caution concerning segmentation and function.

## Minoan names and Linear B continuity

### José Miguel Jiménez Delgado

“Mycenaean names in -to: the Minoan component in the 2nd millennium BC through the lens of anthroponymy” (Ariadne Supplement 5, 2025).

Project use: Linear A -> Linear B name continuities, Minoan/pre-Greek anthroponyms, and suffix/name-family evidence.

Open PDF starting point: https://ejournals.lib.uoc.gr/Ariadne/

### Brent Davis and Miguel Valério

“Names and designations of people in Linear A: A contextual study of tablets HT 85 and 117” (2020).

Project use: contextual identification of likely anthroponyms; `ku-pa3-nu` ~ Linear B `ka-pa3-no`; `ku-pa3-na-tu` ~ `ka-pa3-na-to`; scribal/orthographic cautions.

Record: https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/795936

## Geographic and contextual controls

### Mnamon — Linear A examples and resources

The Scuola Normale Superiore / CNR Mnamon resource provides curated script descriptions and selected inscription examples.

Project use: external control for `SU-KI-RI-TA` (Sybrita) and the likely adjectival derivative `SU-KI-RI-TE-I-JA`; cross-checking which transliterations are purely conventional; links to SigLA, GORILA-related resources, and the PA-I-TO project.

Resource: https://mnamon.sns.it/

### Gournia excavation publication

**“Excavations at Gournia, 2010–2012.”**

Project use: publication context for GO 2 and the hapax `SI-DA-RO`; the discussion compares the form cautiously with Cape Sidero/Sidaro while noting contextual objections. This is used as an alternative hypothesis, not as a secure place-name identification.

### PA-I-TO project / Linear B geographic control

The PA-I-TO epigraphic project documents Linear B `pa-i-to` = Phaistos and corresponding ethnic/toponymic forms.

Project use: positive control for what an independently anchored geographic paradigm looks like. Linear B Greek endings are not projected onto Linear A.

## Specific lexical transmission

### Anna P. Judson

“The Mystery of the Mycenaean ‘Labyrinth’: The Value of Linear B pu2 and Related Signs” (SMEA NS 3).

Project use: `da-pu2-ri-to`, “Lady/Mistress of the Labyrinth,” value of `pu2`, and the possible relationship with Linear A `-DU-PU2-RE`.

Cambridge repository: https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/

## Pre-Greek / substrate etymology

### Robert S. P. Beekes

“Hades and Elysion” (1998).

Project use: Pre-Greek status of Elysion/Eleusis/Eileithyia-related forms; discussion of possible Minoan origin while explicitly distinguishing Pre-Greek from proof of Minoan identity.

PDF: https://www.robertbeekes.nl/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/b90.pdf

### Lotte Meester

“Substrate stratification: An argument against the unity of Pre-Greek” (2024).

Project use: methodological warning against treating all Pre-Greek material as a single language or automatically identifying it with Minoan.

## Earlier methodological / linguistic geography discussion

### Colin Renfrew

“Word of Minos: the Minoan Contribution to Mycenaean Greek and the Linguistic Geography of the Bronze Age Aegean” (Cambridge Archaeological Journal 8.2, 1998).

Project use: the proposal that Minoan contributed substantially to the vocabulary conventionally called “Pre-Greek,” potentially through Bronze Age adstratal contact.

## Citation policy

A lead file should distinguish:

- **data citation:** where the signs/segmentation/scribe attribution come from;
- **interpretive citation:** who has previously proposed a grammatical, lexical, or onomastic interpretation;
- **our inference:** what is newly being tested here.

If a specialist publication already contains the same inference, the repository should credit it and reclassify our result as a replication rather than claim novelty.
