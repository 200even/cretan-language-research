# Lead: final `ME`

**Status:** distributionally interesting; **not currently established as productive morphology by exact-pair evidence.**

## Current claim

Damage-aware v0.3 initially ranked final `ME` #2 among suffixes by accepted exact-paradigm count, with four apparently secure `X ~ X-ME` pairs.

Full inscription/source audit reduces those four to:

| pair | result |
|---|---|
| `A-RA-TU ~ A-RA-TU-ME` | **Tier B candidate** |
| `JA-SA-SA-RA ~ JA-SA-SA-RA-ME` | rejected: cross-face continuation |
| `MA-RU ~ MA-RU-ME` | rejected/reclassified: complex logographic notation flattened into a syllabic string |
| `SA-RA ~ SA-RA-ME` | rejected: cross-face continuation |

Thus only **1/4** v0.3 damage-secure pairs survives as a credible formal morphology candidate.

Full audit: [`../audits/ME.md`](../audits/ME.md).  
Summary: [`../data/me-audit-summary.csv`](../data/me-audit-summary.csv).

## Surviving candidate: `A-RA-TU ~ A-RA-TU-ME`

ZA 7a preserves complete `A-RA-TU 4`. HT Wc 3024 preserves complete `A-RA-TU-ME` on a Haghia Triada roundel with an `OVISᶠ` logogram and six seal impressions.

The formal relationship is real, but:

- it is cross-site;
- it is cross-support;
- common lexical identity is not independently anchored;
- no grammatical role shift can be established.

It therefore remains Tier B.

## Why the other three fail

### Ritual cross-face continuation

Both apparent ritual bases are editorial/layout artifacts:

- IO Za 16 `JA-SA-SA-RA- | -ME` is one word, reconstructed explicitly as `JA-SA-SA-RA-ME`;
- IO Za 12 `JA-SA- | -SA-RA-ME` is likewise one word, so `SA-RA-ME` is not an independent variant.

These cases show why line/face segmentation must be modeled independently from word segmentation.

### `MA-RU-ME` and complex-sign flattening

Bare `MA-RU` on HT 117a is a one-unit personnel/designation-style entry.

The supposed `MA-RU-ME` forms on HT 24a are tabulated by Younger in the **logogram column** as complex `MA+RU ME {*561}` notation. A normalized computational layer converted this into a hyphenated syllabic-looking string and thereby manufactured an apparent `MA-RU + ME` paradigm.

This is not admissible as an ordinary exact suffix pair.

## Relation to ritual scholarship

The recurrent `(J)A-SA-SA-RA-ME` family remains important. Published scholarship, including Orazio Monti, has argued for internal morphology in this ritual sequence, especially the initial `A-/JA-` variation.

That literature does not make the failed exact-pair controls above into secure independent `ME` paradigms.

## Comparison with `JA`

This is now a useful methodological contrast:

- `JA`: seven automatic v0.3 pairs → **two Tier A + two Tier B** after full audit;
- `ME`: four automatic v0.3 pairs → **one Tier B** after full audit.

So `JA` currently has substantially stronger independent-stem paradigmatic support than `ME`, despite both ranking highly in the automatic v0.3 suffix screen.

## What remains plausible

Final `ME` may still be:

- ritual morphology;
- derivational or onomastic morphology;
- part of one or more fixed lexical formations;
- a genuine suffix whose independent paradigms are simply under-attested.

But no productive function is promoted from the current evidence.

## Next evidence needed

Do not assign a function to `ME` until at least one of the following appears:

1. a second independent complete `X ~ X-ME` family surviving type/layout/context audit;
2. a same-tablet or same-scribe contrast;
3. a securely anchored lexical identity showing the same role change;
4. specialist evidence establishing that an apparent complex notation should in fact be read as an ordinary syllabic sequence.
