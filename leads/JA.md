# Lead: productive final `-JA`

**Status:** strong morphological lead; function unknown.

## Claim

Administrative Linear A contains at least two secure bare/extended pairs in which the same final sign `JA` is added to an otherwise identical sign group:

| Base | Extended form | Attestation |
|---|---|---|
| `PA-SE` | `PA-SE-JA` | `PA-SE`: HT 18.1, HT 27b.5; `PA-SE-JA`: HT Wc 3001-3002 |
| `KU-PA` | `KU-PA-JA` | `KU-PA`: HT 110a.2, HT We 1020a, ZA 11a.5/b.3; `KU-PA-JA`: HT 116a.1-2 |

The `KU-PA` family here uses ordinary `PA` and must not be conflated with the distinct `KU-PA3-...` onomastic family tracked elsewhere in this repository.

## Why this matters

This satisfies a stronger criterion than simply finding many words ending in `JA`:

1. the base forms are independently attested as complete words;
2. the extended forms preserve the base exactly;
3. the same extension recurs on two unrelated bases;
4. all of the forms occur in ordinary administrative contexts rather than only in a fixed ritual formula.

The conservative conclusion is therefore:

> `JA` behaves as a productive final morphological element in at least some Linear A administrative vocabulary.

That is a structural claim only. It does not yet establish whether `-JA` is inflectional, derivational, clitic, or orthographic.

## External semantic control

A separate and unusually valuable comparison is:

- `SU-KI-RI-TA` on PH Wa 32;
- `SU-KI-RI-TE-I-JA` on HT Zb 158b.

Mnamon treats `SU-KI-RI-TA` as the toponym Sybrita, known independently from Linear B, and regards `SU-KI-RI-TE-I-JA` as very probably an adjectival derivative of that place name. This does **not** prove that the final `JA` by itself means “from,” “of,” or “Sybritan”; the derivation involves more than a single added sign. It does, however, make origin/association/toponymic derivation a useful pre-registered semantic hypothesis for the next experiment.

## Predictions

If `-JA` participates in a relational or adjectival construction, then a controlled toponym grid should show one or more of the following:

- base place names contrasting with longer `...JA` forms;
- `...JA` forms preferentially occurring with commodities, containers, personnel, or destinations connected to the base place;
- repeated morphological material immediately before `JA` in several toponymic derivatives;
- a distribution consistent across sites and scribes rather than confined to a single hand.

If those predictions fail, `-JA` should remain “productive morphology, function unknown.”
