# Rejected and Downgraded Hypotheses

This file is intentionally prominent. A decipherment project that records only surviving matches is almost guaranteed to overfit.

## 1. Direct Linear A `ELUT-` precursor for Eileithyia / Elysium

**Original idea:** Mycenaean `e-re-u-ti-ja` (Eileithyia) and proposed `e-ru-si-jo` (Elysios?) might preserve a recognizable Linear A `ELUT-/EREU-` stem.

**Test:** search the Linear A corpus for expected internal adjacencies under conservative Linear A -> Linear B sign continuity.

**Result:** no `E-RE`, `RE-U`, or direct `E-RE-U...` sequence was found in the tested corpus. Only `U-TI` was independently recurrent.

**Status:** **rejected as a simple direct-spelling hypothesis.** This does not disprove a Minoan or broader Pre-Greek origin; it rejects the easy orthographic prediction.

## 2. `U-TI` ~ `U-TI-NU` as a clean minimal pair

**Original idea:** ritual `U-TI-NU` might be `U-TI + -NU`.

**Problem discovered:** `U-TI` is independently segmented on HT 10b, but in the ritual formula the relevant signs occur inside the larger segmented word `TA-NA-I-*301-U-TI-NU` (and related variants).

**Status:** **downgraded.** A morpheme boundary before `U-TI` or `NU` remains possible, but the inscriptional segmentation does not prove it.

## 3. Universal N-series declension (`X-NA / X-NE / X-NI / X-NU`)

**Original idea:** final NA/NE/NI/NU might be syllabic spellings of a single consonantal `-N` grammatical suffix.

**Why it looked promising:** raw corpus/graph exploration produced several apparent stem families across n-series endings.

**Controls that weakened it:**

- many apparent forms were subword/relation-graph nodes rather than full inscriptional words;
- some attractive alternations were confined to individual scribes;
- final-vowel selection did not follow a simple preceding-vowel harmony/dummy-vowel rule;
- several surviving N-bearing families are better explained lexically or onomastically.

**Status:** **rejected as a general paradigm.** Individual N-series signs may still be grammatical suffixes in specific constructions.

## 4. `KU-PA3-NU` as strong evidence for generic `-NU` inflection

**Original idea:** secure `KU-PA3` and `KU-PA3-NU` looked like a good bare-stem / suffixed-form pair.

**Counterevidence:** published work compares Linear A `ku-pa3-nu` with Linear B personal name `ka-pa3-no`, and `ku-pa3-na-tu` with `ka-pa3-na-to`. Multiple `KU-PA3-...` forms also coexist in personnel-style lists.

**Status:** **reclassified as an onomastic/lexical family.** It remains highly valuable for Minoan -> Mycenaean continuity, but is weak evidence for ordinary nominal case morphology.

## 5. `PA-RA` ~ `PA-RA-NE` as morphology

**Original idea:** both are real administrative words in broadly comparable recipient-like positions.

**Problem:** the contrast correlated with different Haghia Triada scribes in the validated sample, leaving a scribal/orthographic explanation open.

**Status:** **rejected under the current anti-scribe rule pending stronger replication.**

## 6. `KU-*79-NA` ~ `KU-*79-NI`

**Original idea:** a promising two-syllable stem alternating between N-series endings.

**Problem:** `KU-*79-NI` could be traced to an inscription, while the apparent `KU-*79-NA` hit came from the derived relation vocabulary rather than a verified full inscriptional word.

**Status:** **rejected as a paradigm pair.**

## 7. HT 104 `DA-KU-SE-NE` as an exact repeat of HT 103

**Original observation:** the derived transaction layer listed `DA-KU-SE-NE` on HT 104.

**Raw-transcription check:** HT 104 actually reads `DA-KU-SE-NE-TI`.

**Status:** the derived reading is **rejected**, but the correction produced a stronger lead: cross-scribe `DA-KU-SE-NE` ~ `DA-KU-SE-NE-TI`.

## Principle

When a lead fails, the failure is part of the result. Any future claim should link here when it supersedes an earlier interpretation.
