# Pre-registered experiment: toponymic Kober grid

**Status:** pre-registered; not yet run.

**Registered:** 2026-08-16.

## Research question

Can securely identified or strongly anchored Cretan place names reveal the grammatical function of recurrent Linear A prefixes/suffixes without first identifying the underlying language?

The experiment is specifically intended to test whether the productive final `-JA` lead participates in origin/association/toponymic-adjective morphology and whether other endings form repeatable geographic paradigms.

## Why this is pre-registered

The morphology search has already identified attractive forms. To reduce confirmation bias, the scoring rules and primary anchors are frozen here **before** the toponym corpus is assembled and inspected for outcomes.

## Primary anchor set

### Anchor A: Sybrita

- `SU-KI-RI-TA`, PH Wa 32.
- `SU-KI-RI-TE-I-JA`, HT Zb 158b.

External control: Linear B `su-ki-ri-ta` is a toponym associated with Sybrita. Mnamon regards the longer Linear A form as very probably an adjectival derivative of the toponym.

This is the strongest current positive control for a Linear A place-name derivative.

### Anchor B: Phaistos

- Linear A `PA-I-TO` is attested in the administrative corpus.
- Linear B `pa-i-to` securely denotes Phaistos.

The Linear B corpus provides the useful control condition because Mycenaean Greek forms ethnic/toponymic derivatives of known places. Those Linear B endings are **not** to be projected onto Linear A; they are used only to establish the identity of the place stem and to illustrate what a true geographic paradigm looks like.

## Secondary candidates

Secondary place-name candidates may be added only if they meet one of these conditions before morphological scoring:

1. independent Linear B continuity with a secure geographic identification;
2. external Egyptian/Near Eastern evidence plus a strong Aegean identification;
3. specialist consensus or strong archaeological/epigraphic argument.

Candidates chosen solely because they create an attractive Linear A morphological pair are excluded from the primary test.

## Frozen scoring rules

For each base/derived candidate, record:

- exact Linear A sign sequence;
- inscription ID;
- site and object type;
- scribe where known;
- secure/fragmentary word boundaries;
- preceding and following sign groups;
- associated logogram/commodity;
- numeral/quantity;
- whether the form is a free-standing base or only inferred from a substring;
- external reason for identifying the geographic stem;
- all other corpus words sharing the proposed suffix/prefix.

### Morphological score

A geographic affix hypothesis gains support only when:

- at least two independent geographic stems show the same formal extension;
- the base forms are independently attested or externally anchored;
- the extension is sign-identical, not created by phonetic normalization;
- contexts are compatible with origin, association, destination, possession, ethnicity, or another predeclared relational role;
- the result is not confined to one scribe or one document genre.

### Negative controls

Construct matched non-toponymic stems of comparable sign length and frequency. Measure how often the candidate ending occurs on those controls.

A suffix that appears just as readily on unrelated personal names or commodity terms will **not** be assigned a geographic function merely because it occurs in the Sybrita example.

## Specific predictions

### `-JA`

**H1:** `-JA` participates in relational/adjectival morphology associated with place, origin, or belonging.

Support requires at least one additional geographically anchored family beyond Sybrita whose derived form ends in the same sign `JA` or a demonstrably larger recurrent suffix ending in `JA`.

Failure condition: the administrative `PA-SE/PA-SE-JA` and `KU-PA/KU-PA-JA` pairs remain real morphology, but no geographic enrichment appears. In that case `-JA` remains “productive suffix, function unknown.”

### `SI-DA-*`

**H2:** some `SI-DA-*` forms belong to a geographic or ethnic paradigm.

Support requires contextual or external evidence connecting more than one form to the same place/region. Mere resemblance to Cape Sidero is insufficient.

Failure condition: `SI-DA-RE` behaves as a personal name while `SI-DA-RO` behaves as a place term and ARKH 2 forms occupy unrelated roles. In that case the unified `SI-DA` paradigm is rejected or split into separate lexical families.

## Blinding rule for external-language comparisons

Do not search Sanskrit, Greek, Hurrian, Luwian, Semitic, or Etruscan lexicons while assigning the internal morphological grid. External language comparison begins only after a sign-level geographic pattern survives the controls above.

## Expected output

The experiment should end with one of four outcomes for each candidate affix:

1. **productive morphology + supported geographic function**;
2. **productive morphology + function still unknown**;
3. **lexical/onomastic family rather than general morphology**;
4. **rejected as an overfit pattern**.

No translation of the Minoan language is an expected output.
