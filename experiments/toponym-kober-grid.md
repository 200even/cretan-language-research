# Experiment: toponymic Kober grid

**Status:** completed pilot; both narrow hypotheses failed their pre-registered promotion criteria.

**Registered:** 2026-08-16.

**Run:** 2026-08-16.

## Research question

Can securely identified or strongly anchored Cretan place names reveal the grammatical function of recurrent Linear A prefixes/suffixes without first identifying the underlying language?

The experiment specifically tested whether the productive final `-JA` lead participates in origin/association/toponymic-adjective morphology and whether the wider `SI-DA-*` set forms a repeatable geographic paradigm.

## Why this was pre-registered

The morphology search had already identified attractive forms. To reduce confirmation bias, the primary anchors, scoring rules, support conditions, and failure conditions below were frozen **before** the geographic outcome set was inspected.

## Primary anchor set

### Anchor A: Sybrita

- `SU-KI-RI-TA`, PH Wa 32.
- `SU-KI-RI-TE-I-JA`, HT Zb 158b.

External control: Linear B `su-ki-ri-ta` is a toponym associated with Sybrita. Mnamon and the Heraklion Archaeological Museum regard the longer Linear A form as very probably an adjectival/origin derivative of the toponym; the museum describes the pithos inscription as probably indicating that the vessel came from Sybrita.

This is the strongest current positive control for a Linear A place-name derivative.

### Anchor B: Phaistos

- Linear A `PA-I-TO` is attested in the administrative corpus, including HT 97a.3 and HT 120.6 in concordance/scholarly listings.
- Linear B `pa-i-to` securely denotes Phaistos.

The searchable Linear A corpus contains the base `PA-I-TO` but no `PA-I-TO-...` extended word in the current dataset.

This is therefore a useful **negative geographic control** for any claim that `-JA` or another extension is obligatory on place names.

## Secondary geographic control: `SE-TO-I-JA`

`SE-TO-I-JA` is attested in Linear A on PR Za 1 and the same sequence is independently known as a place name in Linear B administrative geography.

However, the Linear A corpus does **not** supply an independently attested base `SE-TO-I` from which `-JA` can be isolated. It therefore supports the proposition that a geographic word can end in `JA`, but it does not satisfy the pre-registered criterion for demonstrating a productive `X -> X-JA` geographic derivation.

## Frozen scoring rules

For each base/derived candidate, the experiment recorded or checked:

- exact Linear A sign sequence;
- inscription ID;
- site and object type;
- secure/fragmentary word boundaries;
- associated administrative or vessel context where recoverable;
- whether the form is a free-standing base or only inferred from a substring;
- external reason for identifying the geographic stem;
- whether the proposed extension appears on independent non-geographic or semantically unanchored administrative bases.

### Morphological score

A geographic affix hypothesis gained support only if:

- at least two independent geographic stems showed the same formal extension;
- the base forms were independently attested or externally anchored;
- the extension was sign-identical rather than created by phonetic normalization;
- contexts were compatible with origin, association, destination, possession, ethnicity, or another predeclared relational role;
- the result was not confined to one document genre.

### Negative controls

The strongest available administrative controls were the exact pairs already discovered before this experiment:

- `PA-SE` -> `PA-SE-JA`;
- `KU-PA` -> `KU-PA-JA`.

These controls are **semantically unanchored**, not proven non-toponyms. They therefore cannot by themselves demonstrate that `-JA` is non-geographic. They do demonstrate that `JA` is productively attachable outside the one securely identified Sybrita derivation and prevent the Sybrita example from being treated as proof that `JA` alone means “from/of.”

## H1 result: final `-JA` and geographic relation

**Pre-registered H1:** `-JA` participates in relational/adjectival morphology associated with place, origin, or belonging.

**Support condition:** at least one additional geographically anchored family beyond Sybrita whose derived form ends in the same sign `JA` or a demonstrably larger recurrent suffix ending in `JA`.

### Observations

1. **Sybrita is a genuine positive derivative**, but the formal transformation is not `SU-KI-RI-TA + JA`. It is:

   ```text
   SU-KI-RI-TA
   SU-KI-RI-TE-I-JA
   ```

   The relational meaning therefore belongs to some part or all of the larger change `-TA -> -TE-I-JA`; the corpus does not isolate `JA` as the semantic carrier.

2. **Phaistos does not reproduce the pattern.** `PA-I-TO` is securely anchored and attested, but no Linear A `PA-I-TO-...JA` or other extended `PA-I-TO-...` form was found in the current corpus.

3. **`SE-TO-I-JA` is geographically valuable but not segmentable for this test.** Because no independent Linear A `SE-TO-I` base is attested in the current corpus, it cannot constitute the required second `X -> X-JA` geographic pair.

4. **Two exact administrative `X -> X-JA` pairs exist on semantically unanchored stems:** `PA-SE/PA-SE-JA` and `KU-PA/KU-PA-JA`.

### Outcome

**H1 fails its narrow promotion criterion.**

The experiment does **not** support assigning final `JA` the meaning “from,” “of,” ablative, genitive, ethnic, or toponymic adjective.

What survives is stronger structurally but weaker semantically:

> `JA` is productive final morphology in administrative Linear A, and `...JA` participates in at least one independently anchored geographic derivative, but the geographic/relational function cannot be localized to `JA` alone.

A larger suffix such as `-TE-I-JA`, or a stem-conditioned derivational pattern, remains a live hypothesis.

**Classification:** productive morphology + function still unknown.

## H2 result: `SI-DA-*` as a geographic/ethnic paradigm

**Pre-registered H2:** some `SI-DA-*` forms belong to a geographic or ethnic paradigm.

**Support condition:** contextual or external evidence connecting more than one form to the same place/region. Mere resemblance to Cape Sidero was explicitly insufficient.

### Observations

The relevant forms remain:

- `SI-DA-TE` — ARKH 2.1;
- `A-SI-DA-TO-I` — ARKH 2.2-3, on the same tablet as `SI-DA-TE`;
- `SI-DA-RE` — HT 17.3 and HT 122a.5;
- `SI-DA-RO` — GO 2.

The contextual controls do not unify them geographically:

1. The Gournia publication calls Cape Sidero/Sidaro a **tempting** comparison for `SI-DA-RO` but explicitly questions whether Gournia would be auditing disbursements at such a distant location.
2. The same publication notes `SI-DA-RE` in two different contexts: HT 17.3 connected with wine and HT 122a.5 in a personnel list.
3. ARKH 2 securely gives the formal same-tablet pair `SI-DA-TE / A-SI-DA-TO-I`, but no independent evidence links either form to Cape Sidero or to the contexts of the Haghia Triada forms.
4. Published/compiled onomastic comparisons have connected `SI-DA-RE` with a Linear B personal name `si-ta-ro`, which supplies a plausible competing explanation even though this specific continuity still deserves a primary specialist citation before promotion.

### Outcome

**H2 fails.**

The wider `SI-DA-*` collection should **not** currently be treated as one geographic or ethnic paradigm.

The correct split is:

- **surviving Tier A local formal lead:** ARKH 2 `SI-DA-TE` ~ `A-SI-DA-TO-I`;
- **comparison forms only:** HT `SI-DA-RE` and GO `SI-DA-RO` until lexical identity is independently demonstrated.

This is a methodological downgrade, not evidence that the forms are certainly unrelated.

**Classification:** unified geographic paradigm rejected/split; local ARKH 2 morphology survives.

## Overall result

The pre-registered geographic experiment did **not** assign a grammatical meaning to a Linear A suffix.

It did produce two useful constraints:

1. final `JA` is a robust morphological marker but cannot currently be glossed geographically;
2. the visually attractive `SI-DA-{TE,RE,RO}` grid was over-grouped and must be split until contextual identity is demonstrated.

These are valuable negative results because they narrow the search space without relying on a proposed language identification.

## Next experiment suggested by the result

The next best target is **contextual classification of the two exact `-JA` minimal pairs**, not another external-language comparison.

For `PA-SE/PA-SE-JA` and `KU-PA/KU-PA-JA`:

- reconstruct full tablet/roundel contexts;
- identify whether the base and extended forms correlate with person, place, commodity, recipient, origin, or transaction type;
- compare all secure `X/X-JA` pairs corpus-wide;
- use the Sybrita derivative as an external positive control but do not assume its meaning transfers to every `-JA` word.

A second parallel target is direct facsimile/context analysis of the ARKH 2 pair to determine whether `A-` and the ending change correlate with two different administrative roles.

## Final classifications

| Hypothesis | Result |
|---|---|
| `-JA` is productive morphology | **survives strongly** |
| `-JA` alone marks origin/genitive/ablative/toponymic adjective | **not supported** |
| Sybrita has a Linear A geographic adjective/origin derivative ending in `...JA` | **supported by external contextual identification** |
| `SI-DA-*` is one geographic/ethnic paradigm | **rejected/split** |
| ARKH 2 `SI-DA-TE ~ A-SI-DA-TO-I` is a real formal relationship | **survives strongly** |
