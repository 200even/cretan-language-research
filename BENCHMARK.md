# Linear A Morphology Benchmark

**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Frozen:** 2026-08-16  
**Purpose:** a public, auditable benchmark for evaluating structural claims about Linear A morphology without requiring a prior decipherment of the underlying language.

## Research objective

The benchmark is designed to answer a narrower question than “what does Linear A mean?”:

> When two Linear A words look morphologically related, how strong is the inscriptional evidence that they actually instantiate a recurring structural relationship?

The benchmark therefore separates **formal morphology** from **grammatical function** and **translation**. A pair may be excellent evidence that a final sign behaves productively while providing no evidence at all for what that sign means.

## Why a benchmark is needed

Linear A is a small, heterogeneous, partly fragmentary corpus. Attractive patterns can be generated by:

- damaged or restored readings;
- substrings mistaken for complete words;
- derived database nodes that are not inscriptional tokens;
- personal-name and place-name families;
- fixed ritual formulae;
- scribal or regional spelling practices;
- genre-specific vocabulary;
- chance similarity among short CV sequences.

A morphology benchmark is useful only if **negative and failed examples are preserved alongside positive ones**. Otherwise every new hypothesis is tested against a survivor-biased dataset.

## Unit of analysis

The primary benchmark unit is a **relationship between two complete sign groups**, not an isolated sign and not a proposed translation.

Examples:

- exact suffix extension: `PA-SE ~ PA-SE-JA`;
- exact prefix extension: `X ~ A-X`;
- complex-margin relationship: `SI-DA-TE ~ A-SI-DA-TO-I`;
- rejected apparent relationship: a pair created only by a fragmentary reading or derived subword node.

## Required fields

Each benchmark row records:

- `benchmark_id` — stable identifier;
- `base_form`, `variant_form` — exact transliterations, without phonetic normalization;
- `operation` — suffix, prefix, complex, lexical-family, or rejected-artifact;
- `added_material` — sign material implicated by the proposed relationship;
- inscription IDs for both forms;
- site and scribe information where known;
- whether both word boundaries are secure;
- whether either form is damaged/restored;
- whether the contexts are comparable;
- evidence tier;
- adjudication;
- reason for the adjudication;
- provenance/source note.

Machine-readable rows live in [`data/morphology-benchmark.csv`](data/morphology-benchmark.csv).

## Evidence tiers

### Tier A — benchmark positive

The formal relationship is strong enough to use as a positive control:

- both forms are securely segmented complete words;
- the shared material is exact at sign level;
- no undocumented sign substitution is required;
- the relationship is supported by same-tablet/same-scribe evidence or independent replication;
- an obvious simpler explanation such as a database artifact has been excluded.

Tier A does **not** imply that the affix function or lexeme meaning is known.

### Tier B — candidate

The formal relationship is plausible but one important control remains unresolved, such as:

- genre mismatch;
- unknown scribal relationship;
- possible personal-name/toponymic identity;
- uncertain contextual equivalence;
- complex changes on more than one word margin.

Tier B items may generate hypotheses but are not used as unqualified positive controls.

### Tier C — exploratory

A relationship is worth tracking but depends on reconstruction, phonetic normalization, weak context, or an isolated resemblance.

### Rejected / reclassified

The apparent morphological relationship fails a benchmark rule or has a better explanation. Rejected rows remain in the dataset.

Typical reasons include:

- fragmentary reading;
- non-inscriptional derived node;
- scribal confound;
- onomastic/lexical family instead of generic morphology;
- failure of a cross-register prediction.

## Benchmark-positive seed set

Version 0.1.0 intentionally begins small. The strongest current positives are:

1. `PA-SE ~ PA-SE-JA` — exact final `JA` extension in administrative material;
2. `KU-PA ~ KU-PA-JA` — independent exact final `JA` extension;
3. `*21F-TU ~ *21F-TU-NE` — same-scribe bare/extended contrast with cross-scribe replication of the extended form;
4. `DA-KU-SE-NE ~ DA-KU-SE-NE-TI` — cross-scribe exact final `TI` extension.

The ARKH 2 pair `SI-DA-TE ~ A-SI-DA-TO-I` is deliberately **Tier B** because both margins change and contextual equivalence remains to be established.

## Benchmark-negative seed set

Version 0.1.0 also freezes several important failures:

- `U-TI ~ U-TI-NU` as a clean ritual minimal pair — rejected/downgraded because the crucial ritual occurrence is embedded in a larger word and IO Za 11 is fragmentary;
- `PA-RA ~ PA-RA-NE` — rejected under the anti-scribe rule pending independent replication;
- `KU-*79-NA ~ KU-*79-NI` — rejected because one apparent form came from a derived relation vocabulary rather than a verified complete inscriptional word;
- `KU-PA3 ~ KU-PA3-NU` as generic nominal inflection — reclassified as likely onomastic/lexical-family evidence;
- the broad `SI-DA-TE/RE/RO/TO-I` super-paradigm — downgraded after the pre-registered geographic test failed to demonstrate common lexical/geographic identity.

These negatives are as important as the positives for evaluating automatic morphology discovery.

## How computational systems are evaluated

An automatic candidate extractor should be judged on at least four dimensions:

1. **Recovery:** does it rank benchmark-positive relationships highly?
2. **False-positive resistance:** does it avoid promoting benchmark negatives?
3. **Generalization:** does a candidate affix recur across independent stems, scribes, sites, or genres?
4. **Auditability:** can every score be traced back to complete inscriptional words?

No model receives credit for matching a desired external language unless the internal structural prediction was registered first.

## Blinded Davis 2026 replication

Brent Davis’s 2026 Chapter 4 reports a refinement of David Packard’s statistical approach that isolates **two signs strongly associated with word beginnings and four with word endings** as likely grammatical affixes. Our independent ranking procedure is frozen before comparing its output with Davis’s six identities.

The first replication pass uses only:

- exact complete word forms;
- sign position within words;
- exact whole-word `X ~ A-X` / `X ~ X-A` extension pairs;
- no external-language matches;
- no hand-coded knowledge of Davis’s six signs.

Protocol: [`experiments/davis-2026-affix-replication.md`](experiments/davis-2026-affix-replication.md).

## Versioning policy

The benchmark is versioned independently of the exploratory corpus.

A row is never silently changed. If a reading or interpretation changes:

1. record the new source;
2. change the adjudication with an explanation;
3. increment the benchmark version;
4. preserve the rejected/superseded reasoning in git history and, where useful, `REJECTED_HYPOTHESES.md`.

The initial computational substrate remains pinned to `mwenge/lineara.xyz` commit `43fe7cf1abc8e6bb1ea3228c3a1bd5938709620a` until a deliberate corpus-version comparison is run.

## What this benchmark does not claim

It is **not**:

- a new authoritative edition of Linear A;
- a replacement for GORILA or SigLA;
- a translation corpus;
- evidence that conventional Linear B-derived sign values are phonetically exact for Minoan;
- evidence for a particular language family.

The intended contribution is narrower: **make morphological claims easier to reproduce, falsify, and compare.**
